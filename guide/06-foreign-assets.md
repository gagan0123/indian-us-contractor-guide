# Foreign assets: Schedule FA

## The rule

If you are resident in India and **at any time during the year** you
hold an asset outside India, a financial interest in a foreign entity, or
signing authority over a foreign account, you must:

- **file a return**, whatever your income (old s. 139(1) fourth proviso,
  now s. 263(1)(a)(viii)); and
- **report each asset in Schedule FA** of the return, with its peak and
  closing value and any income from it.

**ITR-4 has no Schedule FA.** Holding any foreign asset means filing
ITR-3, even on the presumptive scheme.

## What a contractor is likely to hold

| Item | Report it? |
|---|---|
| Deel payouts withdrawn straight to an Indian bank in INR, nothing left on Deel | Nothing held abroad; arguably nothing to report |
| A **Deel balance** you leave un-withdrawn, a **Deel USD account**, a **Deel Card** | Money held with a foreign entity; treat as reportable |
| **Wise, Payoneer, Revolut** multi-currency balances | Usually treated as reportable |
| **RSUs or ESPP shares** from a previous employer, in an E*Trade, Schwab, Fidelity or similar account | Reportable (vested shares, the brokerage account, and dividends) |
| A US bank account, foreign crypto exchange account | Reportable |

No official guidance names the platform products. Over-reporting costs
nothing; under-reporting is what the penalty is for. Decide with your CA,
and if in doubt, report.

## The reporting period

Schedule FA works on the **calendar year**, not the financial year. In
the return filed in 2026 it covered 1 January to 31 December 2025, so the
return for tax year 2026-27 (filed in 2027) is expected to cover calendar
year 2026; confirm when the forms are notified. Values are converted at
SBI's telegraphic transfer buying rate on the relevant date. For each
account keep:

- the balance on **31 December**;
- the **highest balance** during the calendar year, and its date;
- interest or dividends credited.

Platform apps rarely show a peak balance; download a statement each
December.

## What happens if you miss it

Under the Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and
Imposition of Tax Act, 2015, failing to report a foreign asset carries a
penalty of **₹10 lakh** (ss. 42 and 43). Since 1 October 2024 that penalty
does not apply where your foreign assets other than immovable property
total **₹20 lakh or less** (Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024), and the Finance
Act, 2026 extended the same ₹20 lakh carve-out to prosecution, also from
1 October 2024. The duty to report remains; the carve-out only limits
the penalty.

**FAST-DS 2026.** The Finance Act, 2026 opened a one-time Foreign Assets
of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme for past years, closing **31 December
2026**. An asset bought from income already taxed but left out of
Schedule FA (up to ₹5 crore) can be regularised for a fee of ₹1 lakh;
undisclosed income or assets up to ₹1 crore pay an effective 60 %. If you
have old RSUs or a foreign account you never reported, talk to a CA now,
before it closes.
