# Same company, new role: from the Indian entity's payroll to a contract with the US entity

A common version of this move: you are not leaving the company. You
resign from the **Indian entity's payroll** (a subsidiary, or a branch)
and the **US entity of the same group** engages you as a contractor,
often through Deel, often for the same work, the same manager and the
same team. Everything else in this guide still applies. This chapter
covers the four places where the same-group set-up changes the answer.

## 1. Is it really a contract? (income tax and GST both turn on it)

The label on the agreement does not decide your tax. Indian courts tell
a **contract of service** (employment) from a **contract for service**
(an independent professional) by the substance: who controls how,
when and where you work; whether you are part of the organisation (on
its org chart, in its leave and appraisal systems, with an employee email
signature); whether you can work for others; who bears the cost and risk
of the work; and whether you are paid for time or for results.

If the substance is employment with the US company, your income is
**salary from a foreign employer**: still taxable in India, but

- the **presumptive scheme (s. 58) is not available**, nor are business
  expenses; you get the salary standard deduction instead;
- it is **not a supply for GST** at all (CGST Act, Schedule III, para 1:
  services by an employee to the employer), so no registration or LUT;
- with no Indian TDS, you still pay advance tax yourself.

The same-group move is where this question bites hardest, because
"same work, same manager, same hours" is the employment pattern. You do
not control all the facts, but you control some:

- Read the contract for exclusivity, fixed hours, leave approvals, and
  benefits; ask for terms a contractor would have (deliverables,
  invoicing, your own equipment, freedom to take other work, your own
  insurance).
- Do not keep the trappings of employment: an employee ID, payroll
  benefits, appraisal cycles, an employee title in the signature.
- Invoice and get paid as a business (through Deel), and keep the
  records in `10-records-notices-loans.md`.
- **Put the question to your CA in the first meeting**, with the
  contract, before choosing presumptive and before registering for GST.
  If the answer is "this is employment", the route is a salary return
  with advance tax, and most of chapters 02 and 03 fall away.

The client cares about this too, for its own reasons (whether it has an
Indian tax presence, and labour-law exposure), which is why group
companies often put contractors on Deel and restrict what they may do
for the Indian entity. Those restrictions tend to help your position.

## 2. Who receives the work? (GST: export or not)

Your work is an export only if the **recipient is located outside India**
(IGST s. 2(6)(ii)). For a US company that also has an establishment in
India, the law looks at which establishment receives the service (IGST
s. 2(14)): "where a supply is received at more than one establishment ...
the location of the establishment most directly concerned with the
receipt of the supply".

- **The US entity contracts, pays, and uses the work** (your manager,
  the product and the budget sit in the US entity): the recipient is
  outside India, and the export holds.
- **The work is really for the Indian entity** (you keep working on its
  projects, report into its team, and its budget bears your cost, perhaps
  recharged): the recipient can be located in India. Then it is **not an
  export**, the supply is taxable at **18 % GST**, and the LUT does not
  help.
- If the "Indian branch" is a **branch office of the US company** (the
  same legal entity, not a subsidiary), the establishment question is
  sharper still, because the recipient legally has an establishment in
  India.

What to do: make sure the contract is with, and billed to, the **US
entity**, describes services for the US entity's business, and is paid by
it; know who your manager reports to and which entity's budget carries
your fee; and take the facts to your CA. Keep the evidence (contract,
statements of work, the reporting line) with the year's records.

## 3. Shares from the group's plans: RSUs, ESOPs, ESPP

Group employees often hold unvested **RSUs or options of the US parent**.
On leaving the Indian payroll:

- **Read the plan and your grant letters** for what happens on
  "termination of employment". Many plans lapse unvested awards; some
  let awards keep vesting for a "service provider" or consultant, and an
  intra-group move is sometimes treated as continued service. Ask the
  stock plan administrator in writing.
- **Shares from awards granted while employed** remain a salary
  perquisite even when they arrive after you leave: s. 17(1)(d) of the
  Income-tax Act, 2025 (old s. 17(2)(vi)) taxes specified securities
  "allotted or transferred, directly or indirectly, by the current
  employer, or former employer". The value is the fair market value on
  the date of exercise (for RSUs, in practice, vesting) less what you paid.
  Your former Indian employer usually no longer withholds tax on it, so
  **add it to your advance-tax estimate** in the year of vesting.
- **New equity granted to you as a contractor** is not an employer
  perquisite; it is part of what you are paid for your services, so it
  belongs in your professional receipts. Whether shares as consideration
  meet the GST export payment condition (foreign exchange or permitted
  rupees) is unsettled: ask your CA before accepting equity in place of
  fees.
- **ESPP** participation normally stops when employment ends.
- Every vested share at a US broker is a foreign asset for **Schedule
  FA**, and its dividends and sales are income (`06-foreign-assets.md`).

## 4. The exit from the Indian entity is still an exit

Even though the group is the same, the Indian entity's employment ends,
and everything in `07-leaving-employment.md` applies:

- **Gratuity** is due from the Indian entity if you have five years of
  service with it; service with the US entity as a contractor does not
  continue it.
- **Leave encashment, F&F, Form 130** from the Indian entity.
- **EPF** stops: you are no longer an employee of an establishment that
  covers you. Keep, withdraw or move it as chapter 07 explains.
- **Group health and life cover** end with the Indian payroll, even if
  colleagues in the US are covered by the US entity's plans; contractors
  are normally excluded. Buy your own (`08-money-and-protection.md`).
- **IP and confidentiality**: you sign a new assignment with the US
  entity; check it does not reach back over work you did as an employee
  (that already belongs to the Indian entity) in a way that creates
  conflicting obligations, and that your pre-existing personal work is
  carved out.
- Ask for an **experience or service letter** from the Indian entity and
  an **engagement letter** from the US entity at the same time: lenders
  and consulates will want both (`10-records-notices-loans.md`).

## For your CA, first meeting

1. Contract of service or for service, on these facts?
2. Which establishment is the recipient, and does the export hold?
3. RSUs or options still vesting: perquisite in which year, at what value?
4. Any equity offered as part of the contractor fee: how it is taxed and
   reported for GST.
