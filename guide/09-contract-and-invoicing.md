# The contract and your invoices

## Read these clauses before you sign

The contract is usually the client's template on Deel, sometimes Deel's
own. It is a business-to-business agreement: you have the protections in
it and few others. Read for:

| Clause | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Parties** | Who you contract with: the US client, or a Deel entity. Both are outside India, which keeps the work an export; but you need to know who to invoice and chase |
| **Scope and deliverables** | Defined clearly enough that a dispute about "done" has an answer |
| **Fee, currency, cycle** | USD or INR; monthly fixed, hourly, or milestone; the invoice date and payment terms (eg net 15). Currency decides your exchange-rate risk and the rupee figure in your returns |
| **Fees and charges** | Who bears Deel's fees, withdrawal and exchange costs |
| **Termination** | Notice period on each side. A short notice by the client is your main income risk: size the emergency fund to it |
| **IP assignment** | Normal for the work product. Watch for clauses that take **pre-existing** or unrelated work (your own libraries, side projects). Ask for a carve-out listing them |
| **Non-compete, non-solicit** | Breadth and duration; a broad non-compete can stop you taking other clients |
| **Exclusivity, hours** | Whether you may work for others. A contract that dictates hours, place and tools, with no other clients allowed, looks like employment in substance, and then your income is salary: no presumptive scheme, no GST. It matters most when the client is your former employer's group (`12-same-group-switch.md`) |
| **Confidentiality** | Standard; check it does not bar you from showing the work to your CA or a lender if needed |
| **Liability and indemnity** | A cap, ideally fees paid in the last 12 months. Uncapped indemnity is a real risk for a sole contractor |
| **Governing law, disputes** | Usually a US state; know that enforcement would be costly either way |
| **Leave, holidays, expenses** | Unpaid unless stated; whether expenses are reimbursed and how |
| **Tax clause** | That you handle your own taxes. Fine; make sure it does not ask you to bear the client's US taxes |

## Your own GST invoice

If you are GST-registered, issue a tax invoice for each fee (`03-gst.md`
lists what it must carry). `templates/export-invoice.md` is a fill-in
template. Practical rules:

- **One series per financial year**, eg `EXP/26-27/001`, restarting each
  April; no gaps, no reuse.
- **Upload it to Deel in place of Deel's standard invoice** (Finance →
  Invoices → options menu → Replace with custom invoice), so the client's
  record and your tax invoice are the same document. Match the cycle's
  date and amount.
- **Issue within 30 days** of the service period.
- Keep a **sales register**: a one-line-per-invoice sheet with invoice
  number, date, Deel invoice number, USD amount, INR value used for GST
  and income tax, credit date, UTR, advice received. `templates/
  sales-register.csv` is the starting point. It is your GSTR-1 for the
  quarter and the core of your books.

If you are not registered, Deel's invoice is the commercial document and
you issue no GST invoice; keep the sales register anyway.
