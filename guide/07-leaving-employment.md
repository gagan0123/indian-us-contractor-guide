# Leaving employment: what to collect and what it costs in tax

Do this list in your notice period. Several items are hard to get once
you have left.

## Collect before your last day

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| **Form 130** (formerly Form 16) for April to your last day; your employer issues it after the year ends, so confirm the e-mail it will go to | Salary and TDS for your return |
| The **full and final settlement statement**: last salary, leave encashment, gratuity, notice-period recovery, bonus | Each has its own tax treatment (below) |
| Payslips for the year | Proof of income for loans and insurance while you have no contractor ITR |
| **UAN** and EPF passbook; check KYC (Aadhaar, PAN, bank) is approved on the EPFO portal and the exit date is marked | Without it, withdrawal or transfer later becomes a chore |
| The group **health insurance** end date and the conversion offer | `08-money-and-protection.md` |
| Relieving letter and experience letter | Visas, loans, future employers |
| Any ESOP, RSU or ESPP statements and the plan's rules on leaving | Unvested grants usually lapse; vested ones stay with you and go in Schedule FA if held abroad |
| Your **regime declaration** for TDS | It does not bind your return, but it explains Form 130's figures |

## EPF

The PF balance is **tax-free on withdrawal** if you have **five or more
years of continuous service** (counting earlier employers whose balance
was transferred into the current account), or the balance is transferred
to another employer's fund or NPS (old s. 10(12), now Schedule XI Part A
para 8). Withdrawn earlier, the past years are recomputed as if the fund
were unrecognised: the employer's share and interest become taxable
salary, and TDS of **10 %** applies to a withdrawal of ₹50,000 or more
(old s. 192A, now s. 392(7)).

As a contractor you have no new employer to transfer to. You can:

- **leave it in place**: EPFO's current position is that the account keeps
  earning interest until you reach 58. Interest credited **after** you
  leave employment has been held taxable each year as other income (an
  ITAT ruling; confirm with your CA);
- **withdraw it**: tax-free if you meet the five-year test, taxable
  otherwise; or
- **move it to NPS**: allowed by the rule, though EPFO's process for it is
  limited.

If you are short of five years, keeping it until a future employer
transfer is often the better choice.

## Gratuity

Payable if you have five years of continuous service. Exempt up to the
least of the actual amount, the statutory formula, and **₹20 lakh** over
your lifetime (old s. 10(10), now s. 19(1) Table Sl. 5 and 6). The Labour
Codes in force from 21 November 2025 changed how gratuity is computed
and who qualifies; how the tax exemption reads against them has not been
clarified. Ask your CA if the amount is large.

## Leave encashment

Exempt on leaving up to the least of: leave at credit (at most 30 days
per year of service) times average salary, 10 months' average salary, the
actual amount, and **₹25 lakh** over your lifetime (old s. 10(10AA), now
s. 19(1) Table Sl. 14). Resignation is commonly accepted as "retirement
... or otherwise". Leave encashed while still employed is fully taxable.

## Notice-period recovery

If you leave early and the employer recovers notice pay from you, it is
generally **not deductible** from your salary income; it is cleanest when
the employer nets it off before reporting salary in Form 130. Check what
Form 130 shows.

## Advance tax in the switch year

Your employer deducted TDS on salary only. From your first contract
invoice, the tax on the contract income is yours to pay as advance tax.
See the worked example in `01-income-tax.md`.
