# E-mail: request for the inward remittance advice

Send one per credit, in the first week after the NOC is available, to
your bank's **inward remittance / trade-finance desk** (ask your branch
for the address once; keep it in your notes). Copy your relationship
manager or branch if you have one. Attach the NOC. Fill every `<...>`
from the NOC itself, exactly as it prints them.

---

**Subject:** Request for issuance of FIRA / BIRC | A/c `<account number>` | UTR `<UTR>` (`<DD-Mon-YYYY>`)

Dear Sir / Madam,

I have received an inward remittance in my account for professional
services exported to a client outside India, paid through `<platform>`.
Please issue the Foreign Inward Remittance Advice (FIRA / BIRC) for it.
The correspondent bank's No Objection Certificate is attached.

- Beneficiary: `<your name>`
- Account number: `<account number>`
- Credit date: `<DD-Mon-YYYY>`
- UTR: `<UTR>`
- Transaction reference: `<as on the NOC>`
- Amount: INR `<amount>`
- Remitter: `<as on the NOC>`
- Purpose: `<as on the NOC>`
- Purpose code: `<as on the NOC, eg P0802>`

Please share a scanned copy by e-mail as well as the physical copy.

Regards,
`<your name>`
`<phone>`

---

## Notes

- The NOC is what unblocks the request: it states that the correspondent
  bank has not issued and will not issue the certificate itself. Without
  it, most banks stall.
- The first request can take a week or two; later ones, once the desk
  knows you, often take a day. Reply on the same thread each month so the
  desk sees the history.
- An advice for an INR credit may print no USD amount and no rate. That
  is normal; your invoice carries the foreign-currency figure.
- If the bank says it cannot issue an advice for a rupee credit at all,
  ask for it in writing and take that to your CA: the GST export position
  rests on proof that the money came from abroad (`guide/03-gst.md`).
